AI governance in higher education refers to the responsibility of senior management to formulate policies, establish ethical guidelines, and provide overall institutional direction for AI integration (Chan, 2024). This dimension focuses on ensuring that institutions operate within legal and ethical boundaries while striving for academic excellence and integrity (Chan, 2024). Senior management plays a crucial role in understanding, identifying, and preventing academic misconduct and ethical dilemmas related to AI use (Chan, 2024).

Effective governance requires addressing specific areas such as data privacy, transparency, accountability, and security (Chan, 2024). It involves establishing clear guidelines for the proper attribution of AI-generated content to promote academic honesty and clarity (Chan, 2024). Governance also necessitates implementing measures to ensure equitable access to AI technologies, thereby preventing the exacerbation of existing inequalities among students (Chan, 2024).

The ethical and sustainable development of artificial intelligence depends on good governance, which includes creating standards that are helpful to human society (Singh, 2024). Policymakers and institutions must develop data governance frameworks that protect student privacy and safeguard sensitive data through clear rules on collection and usage (PRESS, 2023). Without sound ethical standards and principles, the smooth implementation of AI in this sensitive sector is not possible (Singh, 2024).

Collaboration among all stakeholders, including universities, teachers, students, and staff, is essential for the successful integration of AI (Chan, 2024). A robust governance framework allows senior management to create a conducive environment for the ethical use of AI, mitigating risks associated with academic misconduct (Chan, 2024). Ultimately, these governance actions contribute to enhanced trust, compliance, and responsible AI usage within the academic community (Chan, 2024).

This governance role involves understanding, identifying, and preventing academic misconduct while addressing data privacy, transparency, accountability, and security concerns (Chan, 2024). Effective governance frameworks are considered essential for promoting a culture of integrity and mitigating risks associated with the misuse of AI technologies in academic settings (Chan, 2024).

Specific governance actions include establishing clear guidelines for attributing AI-generated content to foster transparency and honesty among students and staff (Chan, 2024). Ensuring equitable access to AI resources is also identified as a crucial governance measure to prevent the exacerbation of existing inequalities among students (Chan, 2024). The literature suggests that without sound ethical standards and principles, the smooth implementation of AI in this sensitive sector is not possible (Singh, 2024).

This collaborative approach aims to harmonize pedagogical, governance, and operational dimensions to ensure ethical integration aligned with broader educational goals (Chan, 2024). However, the evidence indicates that issues such as instructors' employability and the moral ramifications of AI often receive insufficient attention despite its expanding use (Singh, 2024).
